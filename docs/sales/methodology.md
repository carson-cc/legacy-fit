# Veltro Methodology One-Pager

## What Veltro measures

Veltro scores candidates on four behavioral dimensions derived from the DISC model — a framework
with over 60 years of applied use in organizational psychology:

| Dimension | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **Dominance** | Drive to control outcomes, directness, competitiveness |
| **Extroversion** | Sociability, expressiveness, need for social stimulation |
| **Patience** | Preference for consistency, deliberateness, tolerance for change |
| **Formality** | Adherence to rules, precision, attention to structure and detail |

Candidates complete a forced-choice behavioral adjective inventory — selecting words that describe
how they naturally operate at work. The inventory presents 20 item pairs; each pair forces a choice
between two adjectives mapped to different dimensions. The format reduces social desirability bias
relative to Likert-style self-report instruments.

## Normative sample

Veltro scores candidates against a normative database of **2.245 million individual profiles**
collected over multiple decades from DISC-instrument administrations across industries, functions,
and geographies. Raw dimension scores are converted to percentiles relative to this norm sample,
producing scores that are stable across populations without requiring role- or firm-specific
calibration.

The normative sample draws on the published research underlying the DISC instrument family,
including the foundational work of William Marston (1928) and subsequent psychometric development.

## 16 behavioral archetypes

Veltro maps each candidate to one of 16 behavioral archetypes (e.g., Pioneer, Conductor, Purist,
Maverick) based on the pattern of their four dimension scores. Each archetype has documented
"works well with" and "may clash with" pairings used for team-fit analysis.

## Fit scoring

When a recruiting firm sets a target profile for a role, Veltro computes a fit percentage using a
weighted quadratic distance function:

```
fit% = 100 × (1 − Σ wᵢ × (candidateᵢ − targetᵢ)²)
```

Weights default to equal (0.25 per dimension) and can be adjusted per job. The quadratic function
penalizes large deviations more than small ones. A perfect match scores 100%; a candidate whose
scores are maximally distant from the target in all four dimensions scores approximately 40%.

## Scientific grounding

The DISC model and forced-choice behavioral inventory format are supported by a substantial
published literature:

- **Barrick & Mount (1991)** — "The Big Five Personality Dimensions and Job Performance: A
  Meta-Analysis." *Personnel Psychology*, 44(1), 1–26. Demonstrates that behavioral/personality
  dimensions predict job performance across criteria and occupational groups.

- **Schmidt & Hunter (1998)** — "The Validity and Utility of Selection Methods in Personnel
  Psychology: Practical and Theoretical Implications of 85 Years of Research Findings."
  *Psychological Bulletin*, 124(2), 262–274. Establishes that structured assessments add
  incremental validity over unstructured interviews.

These meta-analyses support the general class of behavioral assessment tools. Veltro's specific
instrument has not been the subject of an independent criterion-validity study.

## What Veltro does NOT claim

Veltro is transparent about the following limitations:

1. **No independent criterion-validity study.** Veltro has not conducted a formal study correlating
   its assessment scores with job performance outcomes (e.g., sales quota attainment, 90-day
   retention, manager ratings). The assessment is grounded in a well-validated framework but the
   specific instrument has not been independently validated.

2. **No adverse-impact monitoring by default.** Veltro does not monitor or report adverse-impact
   statistics across demographic groups unless the client explicitly opts in to demographic data
   collection. Clients are responsible for conducting their own adverse-impact analyses if legally
   required.

3. **Not a hiring decision.** Veltro produces a behavioral profile and fit score — a data point for
   human decision-makers, not an automated hiring decision. Clients are responsible for all hiring
   decisions and for complying with applicable employment law.

4. **Self-report limitations.** Like all self-report instruments, Veltro's adjective inventory is
   susceptible to faking if a candidate deliberately misrepresents their behavior. The forced-choice
   format reduces (but does not eliminate) this risk.

## Recommended use

Veltro is most defensible when used as **one structured input** alongside structured interviews,
reference checks, and work-sample assessments — not as a standalone screening filter.

Clients using Veltro as the sole basis for candidate elimination should consult employment counsel
before doing so.
